How to play Forkline, and what it teaches

Forkline is a free, turn-based space trading game that plays in any browser on a phone or a PC. You start with a ship and a loan. Buy goods where they are made, sell them where they are wanted, and clear the debt before the interest grows. A kid can play it in ten minutes; adults keep coming back to beat their own fund.

How it plays

Five steps. The Beginner mission walks you through all of them with a coach that explains why each move works.

  1. Look at the market. Each stop sells a few goods. The price tells you whether it is cheap or dear here, and the coach says what to buy.
  2. Buy where it is made. Water is cheap at an ice mine. Electronics are cheap where they are built. Fill your hold with what the next stop wants.
  3. Fly to where it is wanted. Every jump takes days. Stops in the Solar System are close; the stars pay more but take longer, and their price reports arrive days old.
  4. Sell, and watch the price move. Sell a little and you get the full price. Flood a stop with one good and it pays less for a while. The Compare view shows every stop's price for one good side by side.
  5. Clear your debt. Your loan grows a little every day. Pay it down at a Halyard stop, keep trading, and grow your fund. When the mission ends, your fund is cash minus debt.

What it teaches

Nothing in Forkline is a lecture. Each idea below is something the game makes you feel in the first mission, and the Codex inside the game explains it in plain words when you want more.

Supply and demand

A good is cheap where it is made and dear where it is wanted. The whole game is noticing that gap and carrying cargo across it.

In the game: the market shows "cheap here" or "dear here" with the stop's usual range.

Price impact

Selling a lot at once lowers the price you get. Real markets work the same way, from farm crops to used cars.

In the game: a "flooded" tag and a lower price after a big sale, recovering a little each day.

Debt and compounding

A loan grows every day whether you trade or not. Paying early saves money; waiting costs more than it looks.

In the game: the debt line grows daily, and the coach reminds you to pay it down before the end.

Opportunity cost

A long trip that pays more is not always the better trip. What matters is profit per day, because days are the thing you cannot buy.

In the game: Compare ranks trips by profit per day, so a quick hop can beat a star run.

Old information

Prices from the stars arrive days old. Deciding on stale numbers is a real skill, and so is knowing how stale they are.

In the game: star rows say "Price report: 2d old" next to the price.

Planning under a deadline

Missions last 10, 30, 45 or 60 days. A plan that needs 12 days on day 50 is not a plan.

In the game: the goal bar shows your fund against the target, and the day counter shows what is left.

For families and classrooms

How parents, homeschoolers and teachers use Forkline today, with no account and no setup.

Hand it to a kid

The Beginner mission is 10 days of game time, about 10 minutes of play, with a coach on every step. Start it from the home card marked "New here?". The goal is simple: grow the fund from $10M to $30M.

Play the same game together

The Daily Mission gives every player the same markets for the day, so a family or a class can trade the same goods and compare results. Nobody gets a different deal.

Talk about it afterwards

The end screen recaps what you bought, what you sold and the profit on each good. Three questions that work: Which trade made the most per day? What would you do differently? Why did the price drop when you sold a lot?

We are looking for two classrooms or homeschool co-ops to run a short pilot with the current game and tell us what a class needs. If that is you, write to hello@playforkline.com. No student information is needed or collected.

Safe by design

Children play Forkline, so the rules are strict and the list is short.

  • Built to an E for Everyone standard: no violence, no gambling, no mature themes
  • No ads of any kind today
  • No account, no login, no email to play
  • Your save stays in your browser on your device
  • The game sends anonymous daily totals only (for example how many missions started), with no player ID, device ID or cookie
  • Tester reports ask for a callsign, never a name; under 14 needs a grown-up's OK

Full details are on the tester report page, and the whole build process is public on the case study. Questions: hello@playforkline.com.

Questions

Is it really free?
Yes. The full game is free. We plan an optional one-time unlock for longer missions later; the full mission stays free.
What devices does it run on?
Any modern browser on a phone, tablet or PC. There is nothing to download. On a phone you can add it to your home screen from the browser menu.
How long is a mission?
Beginner is 10 game days, about 10 minutes. Full missions are 30, 45 or 60 days, roughly 20 to 40 minutes, and you can stop and come back; the save is on your device.
Is it a stock market game?
No. You trade cargo between places, like the classic buy-low, sell-high games. There is no stock ticker, no gambling and no real money.
Can my class use it?
Yes, today, with no account: every player opens the same link. The Daily Mission is identical for everyone. A teacher-controlled class version is something we are exploring; the pilot note above is the first step.
Who makes it?
Songtan Labs, a one-person studio run by Jason Von Holmes, with a team of AI agents doing most of the engineering and a tester crew credited by callsign in the game.
